## Conclusion

TSMC's 2025 3nm ramp is primarily a **capacity and variant-transition story**: the company is adding 3nm fabs in Taiwan, Arizona and Kumamoto, converting 5nm lines to 3nm, and pushing toward a ~180,000 wafer-per-month 3nm target, while the N3X variant entered volume production in 2025 on top of N3E/N3P [8][9]. On products, the evidence firmly ties Apple's A17 Pro, A18 Pro and M4 family, plus Intel's Arrow Lake/Lunar Lake GPU tiles and (planned) AMD Zen 5 variants to TSMC 3nm — but most of these product attributions are dated to 2024, and **the evidence does not give a reliable, 2025-specific list of 3nm products**.

## The 2025 ramp

**Capacity expansion.** TSMC is "currently adding three new 3nm fabrication plants in Taiwan, Arizona, and Kumamoto, Japan, while continuing to convert existing 5nm production lines to support additional 3nm capacity" [9]. Strong order momentum from Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom is expected to help TSMC hit its year-end target of 180,000 3nm wafers per month ahead of schedule, possibly as early as Q4 [9]. Earlier reporting described Fab 18 (Tainan) phases 4–6 as dedicated to the N3 family, with Fab 12 phases 8–9 and Arizona Fab 21 Phase 2 (30,000 of 50,000 planned monthly wafers on N3) also involved [3]. Japan's second Kumamoto fab has received government approval to produce advanced 3nm chips [11].

**Utilization and pricing.** N5/N4 and N3 capacity utilization is described as "full," with utilization expected to exceed 100% in the second half of the year and strong demand projected to continue into 2025 [7]. The same report indicates N3 production and packaging prices rising by up to 20% [7].

**Node variants.** N3E began mass production in Q4 2023 and N3P in Q4 2024 [1]; N3P offers ~5% higher performance than N3E at the same power, or 5–10% lower power with 1.04× density [1][2]. N3X, tailored for HPC, was scheduled for H2 2025 [1][2] and, per TSMC's own page, "started volume production in 2025" [8]. N3C (value-tier) and N3A (automotive) round out the family, with N3C volume production listed as 2026 [1][8]. TSMC's 3nm capacity grew more than 300% in 2024 versus 2023, providing the base for the 2025 ramp [1].

**Context.** TSMC reported ~$33.1B revenue in Q3 2025, up ~41% year-over-year, with advanced nodes the bulk of wafer revenue and leading-edge production "moving from 3nm into 2nm production lines" [6].

## Products using 3nm

- **Apple:** the A17 Pro is manufactured on N3 [1]; Apple moved to the A18 Pro for iPhone 16 and the M4 series for Macs on 3nm [4], with 3nm-class wafers for A17/A18-series processors priced at ~$18,000 [5].
- **Intel:** expected to use TSMC 3nm for the GPU tile in Arrow Lake and Lunar Lake, and possibly compute tiles [4].
- **AMD:** reportedly tapping the node for Zen 5 processors [4]; Zen 5 was designed with both 4nm and 3nm in mind, is currently fabricated on N4P, and is "planned to be fabricated on the N3E process in the future" [12].
- **Nvidia:** notably *not* a 3nm customer in the 2024 timeframe — Blackwell uses N4P instead [4]. Nvidia's Rubin platform is expected to run on TSMC advanced process technology with orders through 2027, but the evidence does not specify 3nm [11]; Nvidia appears among the customers driving 3nm order momentum in [9].
- **HPC/automotive/value-tier:** N3X targets HPC, N3A targets automotive, N3C targets value-tier products [1][8].

## Caveats and conflicts

- **Product evidence is largely 2024-dated.** Sources [4] and [5] describe 2024 product transitions; [12] describes Zen 5's 3nm use as planned/future. No source enumerates which specific shipping products use 3nm *in 2025*. The available evidence is insufficient to answer that part precisely.
- **Date ambiguity in [9]:** the article is dated 2026 and refers to "this year," so it is unclear whether the 180,000 wafer/month target and the three new fabs pertain to 2025 or a later year. Treat those figures as directionally indicative rather than confirmed 2025 numbers.
- **N3X timing:** [1] and [2] frame N3X as *scheduled* for H2 2025, while TSMC's own page [8] states it *started* volume production in 2025 — consistent, but [8] is the more definitive claim.
- **Nvidia's node:** [4] explicitly contradicts the assumption that Blackwell is 3nm; any claim that Nvidia's 2025 AI chips are 3nm is not supported here.
